One rider.Every venue.

Build your rider once, from stage plot and risers to lighting, video and pyro. Send it to any venue in their language and units. Plan every changeover of a tour or festival, down to which mic stays on stage.

Free and open source. No account, no subscription. Works on your phone, and offline once installed.

How it works

  1. BuildStart from a template or import your old rider. Drag gear on the stage, fill in inputs and monitors, add what your show uses.
  2. CheckLook at the finished rider in the venue's language and units, check it against the venue's gear, see what changed since last time.
  3. SendDownload the PDF, send it by email or a link, or work on it together with your band and tour manager.

Stage plot

Drag gear onto the stage. Grid squares are 1 metre (2 ft in imperial). Click something to name, colour, turn, resize or layer it; arrow keys nudge it. Everything moves on its own: to move things together (a drum riser with the kit), Shift-click or drag a box around them and press Group (Ctrl+G). Ungroup (Ctrl+Shift+G) splits them again. Click a group once to select it all, click again to change just one part, like the drum kit.

Cable list

Made from your stage plot and input list: the XLR length for every line, mic stands, and power cables to every drop. Put the stageboxes on the stage plot for exact lengths.

Rigging

The weight on every truss and every motor point, worked out from your lighting plot: fixtures, drops, LED walls and the truss itself. An estimate for planning: the venue's rigger always checks the final numbers.

Fixture library

Search several hundred fixtures by maker or name and add them to your fixtures with all their DMX modes. Fixture data from the Open Fixture Library (MIT licence), made by its community: check channel counts against the manual before the show.

Security & barriers

The crowd barrier in front of the stage, gates, fences and security guards. The barrier is the Dutch type used at most festivals: an A-frame with a foot plate on the audience side, so the crowd's own weight holds it down, and a step on the pit side for the security team.

Security plot

The area in front of the stage is drawn below the stage. Drag barriers, gates, fences and guards to move them. The stage shows faded underneath.

Barriers, fences and guards

Risers

Drum risers, keys and band risers, dancer platforms, catwalks and steps. Set the size, height, top, skirt, steps and rails, and whether it rolls and how it locks. TheRider works out the decks and legs to build it.

On the stage

Drag a riser to move it. Gear only moves along when you've grouped it with the riser (on the stage plot: select both, then Group). Click a riser to jump to its settings. Arrow keys nudge the selected riser.

Backdrops

Backdrops, kabuki drops (a drop that falls on a cue), scrims and banners. Add as many as you use; place them on the lighting plot. Set the size, where the top hangs and what it's made of.

Backdrop plot

Drag a backdrop to move it; trusses show faded so you can see where it hangs. Click one to jump to its details.

Video & LED walls

LED walls, projection screens, projectors (beamers) and TVs. Place them on the lighting plot; set the size, height, signal and who brings what.

Video plot

LED walls and screens with their size, projectors with their throw to the nearest screen. Drag to move; the stage and lighting rig show faded underneath.

Operator & content

Effects & pyro

CO2 jets, flame units, cold sparks, confetti, pyro and low fog. Place them on the lighting plot and give each one a cue and an operator.

Flames and pyro need permission

They need a licensed operator, approval from the venue and often a permit from the local authority or fire service. Always tell the venue well before the show.

Effects plot

Every effect with its cue and a keep-clear circle. Set the real safety distance per effect below; a * means a planning guess. Drag to move.
